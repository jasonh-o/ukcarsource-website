Why Are UK Prestige Cars Cheaper Than Overseas Dealers?

Buyers from Kenya to Singapore, and from Jamaica to Dubai, often find that the Rolls-Royce, Range Rover or Bentley they want costs less to buy in the UK than from their local dealer. This guide explains why, and when it is worth it.

Get a Quote

The United Kingdom is one of the best places in the world to source a prestige vehicle. For many buyers the UK price, plus shipping, is lower than the local dealer price for the same car. Whether that is still true after your own country's import taxes depends on where you are and which car you want, which is why we quote every vehicle individually.

Five things work in the export buyer's favour. Not all of them apply to every car or every market.

Reason 1: UK VAT and Export Pricing

UK showroom prices include VAT at 20%. A new or VAT-qualifying vehicle can be sold without UK VAT when it is exported and HMRC's conditions are met, so on those cars the export price can be noticeably lower than the UK showroom price.

For used vehicles, the position is different. Most used prestige cars in the UK are sold under the VAT Margin Scheme, which means VAT is only charged on the dealer's margin, not the full value of the car. That margin VAT can also be zero-rated on export if HMRC's conditions are met, but the saving is small. Some used cars are sold as VAT-qualifying, usually those previously owned by a business, and these can carry a larger saving. We tell you which applies on every quote.

Reason 2: Competitive UK New Car Pricing

Manufacturers set prices separately for each country. The UK is one of the largest markets in the world for prestige brands, with many dealers competing for buyers, and that competition helps keep prices and deals sharp.

In smaller markets, such as Sri Lanka, Bangladesh or Jamaica, there may be only one official importer and far less choice. Local prices also carry that country's import taxes and the importer's costs, which is why the same car often costs more there.

Some prestige cars are built in the UK, including Rolls-Royce at Goodwood, Bentley at Crewe and Range Rover in Solihull. In the UK their prices carry no import duty. Sold through a dealer abroad, the same car carries that country's import taxes and the local dealer's margin. In Singapore, for example, that means excise duty, registration fees and a Certificate of Entitlement on top of the price.

Reason 3: Exchange Rates

UK cars are priced in pounds sterling. When sterling is weaker against your currency, the same UK car costs you less in your own money, and when it is stronger, it costs more. Rates move all the time, so we quote in GBP and you can compare the price against your local options on the day you decide.

Reason 4: Depth of the UK Used Prestige Car Market

The UK has one of the world's largest and most active used prestige car markets. High levels of car finance, regular renewal cycles and a large company car fleet mean a steady supply of lightly used, well-maintained luxury vehicles.

At any one time there is usually a wide choice of:

  • Range Rover Autobiography and Range Rover Sport across several model years
  • Bentley Bentaygas and Continental GTs in different specifications
  • Porsche Cayennes, from the standard model up to the Turbo GT
  • Rolls-Royce Cullinan, Ghost and Wraith from recent years

With that much choice, buyers can be selective on specification, mileage and history, and competition between sellers keeps used prices realistic.

Many UK used cars also come with full service histories, often from official franchise dealers, and cars over three years old have an MOT history you can check online.

Reason 5: Right-Hand Drive

The UK drives on the left, so prestige cars here are built and sold in right-hand drive. That matters to the more than 70 countries and territories that also drive on the left.

For buyers in Kenya, Singapore, Malaysia, Jamaica, Barbados, Cyprus, Malta, Sri Lanka, Tanzania, Uganda, Zimbabwe and many others, the UK is a natural source of right-hand drive prestige cars. Japan is the other large right-hand drive source, but its cars are built to Japanese specification. Cars from mainland Europe are left-hand drive, and converting a modern prestige car is rarely practical.

Few other right-hand drive markets offer the same depth of stock in brands such as Rolls-Royce, Bentley, Range Rover and Porsche.

What About Import Duties?

Import duties and local taxes are real, and they are paid locally by the buyer, so check current rates with your clearing agent. The question is whether the UK price and shipping, plus your local taxes, comes to less than buying the same car at home.

In many markets it does, especially on higher-value cars and on models that are hard to find locally. Where total taxes are very high, as in Indonesia and Thailand on large engines, the saving is smaller, although buyers often still import because the exact car they want is not available locally.

We quote CFR to your port, or CIF including marine insurance on request, so you can add your local taxes and compare like for like before you commit.

Which Vehicles Offer the Best Value from the UK?

The difference tends to be largest on higher-value vehicles:

  • Rolls-Royce (Cullinan, Ghost, Phantom, Spectre): built in the UK, with a strong used market of recent cars.
  • Bentley (Bentayga, Continental GT, Flying Spur): built in the UK, with good used market depth.
  • Range Rover (Autobiography, SV, Sport): built in the UK, one of the most popular prestige cars for export, and widely available in right-hand drive.
  • Porsche (Cayenne, Panamera, 911): a strong UK used market with a wide choice of specification.
  • Mercedes-AMG (G63, S-Class, GLS): an active UK used market, and the G63 is in strong demand in many African and Asian markets.
