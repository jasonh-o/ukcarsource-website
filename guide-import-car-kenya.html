Import Guide: Kenya

How to Import a Car from the UK to Kenya

Kenya drives on the left, so UK right-hand drive cars suit its roads with no conversion. The main rules are the 8-year age limit, a KEBS inspection before the car ships, and customs clearance in Mombasa. This guide explains each step and what to check before you buy.

Why UK Cars Suit Kenya

Kenya drives on the left, so a UK right-hand drive car needs no steering conversion. The UK also has a deep market in the models Kenyan buyers ask for, from Range Rover and Land Cruiser to Mercedes-Benz and Porsche.

The process is well established: the car must meet the 8-year rule, pass a KEBS inspection before it leaves the UK, and be cleared by KRA in Mombasa through your clearing agent.

Age Restriction: The 8-Year Rule

Kenya's most important rule for vehicle importation is the 8-year age limit. Vehicles must be not more than 8 years old from the year of first registration (not the year of manufacture) at the time the vessel departs the UK port.

8-Year Rule: Practical Examples (for 2026 imports)

For imports in 2026, the vehicle's first registration date must be no earlier than:

1 January 2019 (i.e. vehicles first registered in 2019 or later qualify)

A vehicle first registered in December 2018 does NOT qualify for import to Kenya in 2026. It is more than 8 years old.

Important: The age is calculated from the V5C first registration date, not the manufacture date. A vehicle built in 2018 but first registered in March 2019 would qualify.

The 8-year rule is long-standing and is checked on arrival. A car that arrives in Mombasa over the age limit can be refused, which is a costly mistake. We verify every vehicle's registration date against current qualifying years before accepting any Kenya export order.

The Taxes KRA Charges

Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) charges several taxes on an imported car. They are worked out on the customs value, which KRA takes from its Current Retail Selling Price (CRSP) list rather than the price you paid:

  • Import duty
  • Excise duty, which goes up with engine size
  • VAT at 16%
  • Import Declaration Fee (IDF)
  • Railway Development Levy (RDL)

The rates have changed several times in recent Finance Acts, so your clearing agent confirms the current figures for your car before you buy. We quote CFR Mombasa, or CIF including marine insurance on request. Duty and local taxes are paid by the buyer on arrival.

KEBS Pre-Export Inspection

Kenya requires all used vehicles imported from the UK to undergo a Kenya Bureau of Standards (KEBS) pre-export inspection before the vessel departs the UK. This inspection is mandatory and must be arranged in advance.

The inspection is carried out in the UK by the inspection company KEBS has appointed. It typically covers:

  • Vehicle identity verification (VIN/chassis number matches V5C)
  • Overall condition assessment: body, interior, mechanical
  • Road safety check (tyres, brakes, lights, windscreen)
  • Odometer reading recorded
  • Checks that the vehicle has not been written off
  • Photographic record of the vehicle's condition

The car must pass before it is loaded. If it fails, for damage, condition or a document mismatch, it cannot ship to Kenya until the issue is put right.

The KEBS inspection certificate is a mandatory document for Mombasa customs clearance. Without it, KRA will not release the car.

Mombasa Port: Clearance Process

All vehicle imports to Kenya enter through Mombasa Port on Kenya's Indian Ocean coast.

The clearance process at Mombasa involves:

  1. KRA customs declaration: Filed electronically with KRA by your clearing agent.
  2. Document verification: KRA reviews all import documents including V5C, Bill of Lading, KEBS certificate, commercial invoice, and marine insurance certificate.
  3. Customs valuation: KRA assesses the dutiable value. Kenya uses a customs value database (CRSP: Current Retail Selling Price) for used vehicles. If the declared purchase price is below the CRSP, KRA will assess duties on the CRSP value, not the declared price. This can result in higher duties than expected if the vehicle was purchased below market value.
  4. Duty payment: Duties and taxes are paid to KRA. Payment can be made in Kenya shillings.
  5. Release and examination: The container is released for physical examination at the port. Vehicle condition is checked against the KEBS certificate.
  6. Exit from port: Vehicle is cleared and can be transported to Nairobi or elsewhere in Kenya.

Using a reputable Mombasa clearing agent is strongly recommended. The KRA process has strict document requirements, and an experienced agent makes sure nothing is missed.

RHD Advantage

Kenya drives on the left. UK right-hand drive vehicles require no modification, no steering conversion, and no headlight adjustment. They arrive in the correct configuration for Kenyan roads, giving direct access to the full depth of the UK prestige vehicle market without the complication or cost of LHD conversion.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

  • Age limit failure: Buying a vehicle that is older than 8 years from first registration is the single most costly mistake. Always verify V5C first registration date and calculate age from that date.
  • Not booking the KEBS inspection early: book it as soon as the car is bought, not the week before shipping.
  • Undervaluing the vehicle: KRA uses CRSP database values and will assess duty on the higher figure. Attempting to undervalue imported vehicles is a customs offence.
  • Missing documents: A missing KEBS certificate or incorrect Bill of Lading can cause the vehicle to be held at Mombasa Port, accruing storage charges. Prepare all documents before the vessel departs the UK.
  • Not using a Mombasa clearing agent: KRA customs in Mombasa requires local expertise. Use a reputable agent with experience handling UK vehicle imports.

Frequently Asked Questions

How old can a car be to import into Kenya?

No more than 8 years from the year of first registration. For cars shipped in 2026, that means first registered in 2019 or later.

What taxes will I pay on a car imported into Kenya?

Import duty, excise duty by engine size, VAT at 16%, the Import Declaration Fee and the Railway Development Levy, worked out on KRA's CRSP value. Your clearing agent confirms the current rates for your car.

Who inspects a used car from the UK for Kenya?

The inspection company appointed by the Kenya Bureau of Standards (KEBS) inspects the car in the UK before it ships. The certificate is needed for clearance in Mombasa.
