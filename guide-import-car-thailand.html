Import Guide — Thailand

How to Import a Car from the UK to Thailand

Thailand's luxury car market is vibrant — Bangkok has some of the highest concentrations of ultra-luxury vehicles in Southeast Asia. But importing from the UK involves navigating some of the highest tariff rates in the region, a preference for left-hand drive in the prestige segment, and strict CBU import regulations.

Overview — Thailand and Vehicle Imports

Thailand is a left-hand traffic country (traffic drives on the left) where RHD vehicles are used domestically. This would suggest UK RHD vehicles are a direct fit — and indeed, for domestic Thai models, RHD is standard. However, the prestige and luxury import market in Thailand operates differently: for most imported European luxury vehicles, LHD versions are imported as CBU (Completely Built Up) units, often because the manufacturer's LHD production is larger, or because the buyer associates LHD with European prestige.

This creates an unusual dynamic: despite Thailand driving on the left, buyers of European luxury imports — Range Rover, Porsche, Bentley, Rolls-Royce — often purchase LHD vehicles. UK vehicles are therefore less commonly sourced for Thailand compared to markets like Kenya or Singapore, but they are imported, particularly when the specific specification or model is not available in left-hand drive.

Thailand's import duties on luxury vehicles are extremely high — among the highest in Southeast Asia — which substantially inflates the landed cost of any imported vehicle regardless of origin.

Import Duty Structure

Thailand imposes a layered tax system on CBU vehicle imports. The rates vary by engine displacement and vehicle type:

TaxRateNotes
Customs / Import Duty80%On CIF value. Applies to cars with engine >2,400cc from non-FTA countries.
Excise Tax30–50%+Applied to the duty-inclusive price. Varies by engine size and CO2.
Interior Tax10% of ExciseAdditional interior tax on excise base.
VAT7%Applied to the duty + excise inclusive price.
Health Tax2% of ExciseAn additional health levy on excise value.

The combined effect is dramatic. For a large-engine luxury car (3.0+ litre) from a non-FTA country like the UK, the total tax burden can reach 200–250% of the CIF value. A vehicle purchased in the UK for £100,000 might face total duties of £200,000–£250,000, creating a landed cost in Thailand of £300,000–£350,000 before registration and local fees.

Thailand has Free Trade Agreements with ASEAN member nations and a few others — but the UK is not currently an FTA partner, so standard MFN (Most Favoured Nation) rates apply, which are the maximum rates above.

Why People Still Import Despite the Duties

The economics only work for very high-value vehicles where no locally-available equivalent exists, or where the specific specification (bespoke commission, limited edition, or unique configuration) commands a premium that absorbs the duty burden. A Rolls-Royce Cullinan or Bentley Bentayga Mulliner in a unique bespoke specification simply cannot be purchased locally in Thailand — buyers who want exactly that vehicle will import regardless of cost.

LHD vs RHD in Thailand

While Thailand technically drives on the left (meaning RHD is legally used and the infrastructure supports it), the prestige import market has strong LHD conventions:

  • All authorised dealer imports of European luxury cars to Thailand are LHD. Dealers import LHD because European manufacturers produce more LHD units globally and the supply chain is optimised for it.
  • Resale value for imported vehicles in Thailand is higher for LHD, as buyers are accustomed to LHD prestige cars.
  • RHD can be imported — it is legal — but the practical consequence is that resale is more limited and some buyers perceive RHD as an unusual configuration for a prestige import.
  • For buyers who intend to keep the vehicle long-term and do not need resale liquidity, RHD from the UK can still work well.

CBU Import Regulations

Thailand requires all imported passenger vehicles to be CBU — Completely Built Up, meaning the vehicle is imported as a finished unit, not as component parts for assembly. All our vehicle exports satisfy this requirement, as we export complete finished vehicles. There is no requirement to disassemble or modify the vehicle before shipment.

Importers must be registered businesses or licensed importers for commercial quantities — private individuals importing a single vehicle for personal use must use a licensed importer as their import agent. The importer of record takes responsibility for duty payment and compliance.

Age Restrictions and Condition Requirements

Thailand does not have a blanket vehicle age restriction in the same way as Kenya, but Thai customs assesses vehicle condition and will apply customs value based on Thai market equivalents rather than simply accepting the declared purchase price. Condition certificates, full service histories, and accurate documentation are important to avoid disputes over customs valuation.

Most Popular Imported Models in Thailand

Despite the extremely high duty rates, the following vehicles are regularly imported to Thailand's luxury market:

  • Range Rover (all trims): Very popular in Bangkok. The Autobiography and SV variants are status vehicles among Thailand's affluent class.
  • Porsche Cayenne Turbo and Turbo S E-Hybrid: High-specification Cayennes are sought-after.
  • Rolls-Royce (Cullinan, Ghost, Wraith): Ultra-luxury buyer market in Bangkok is active.
  • Bentley Bentayga and Continental GT: Popular with Thailand's ultra-high-net-worth buyers.
  • Mercedes-AMG G63 and S63: Strong demand for high-performance AMG models.
  • BMW M models: M5, M8 Grand Coupe, and X5M are imported regularly.

Shipping — Laem Chabang Port

The main port of entry for vehicles imported to Thailand is Laem Chabang Port, located approximately 130 km south-east of Bangkok in Chonburi Province. It is one of Southeast Asia's largest container ports and handles vehicle imports efficiently.

Transit time from UK ports to Laem Chabang is typically 25–35 days by container, usually via transhipment at Port Klang (Malaysia) or Singapore. We recommend dedicated 20ft container shipping for all luxury prestige vehicles imported to Thailand given the vehicle values involved.

Documentation Required

  • UK V5C (vehicle registration document)
  • Original purchase invoice
  • Bill of Lading
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Marine insurance certificate
  • Certificate of Origin (confirming vehicle is of UK origin)
  • Manufacturer's Certificate of Conformity (if available)
  • Thai customs importer registration / licensed importer appointment letter
