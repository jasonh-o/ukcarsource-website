How to Import a Car from Japan to the UK

Published May 2026 · UK Car Source

Japan remains one of the world's most compelling sources for specialist, performance and collector vehicles — particularly right-hand drive models unavailable elsewhere. Japanese market cars typically have meticulous service histories, lower-than-average mileage and excellent condition, making them sought after by UK buyers seeking something rare or specific.

Importing from Japan involves several stages: identifying the vehicle through a trusted auction agent or dealer network, arranging export documentation from Japan, sea freight to a UK port (typically Tilbury, Southampton or Bristol), DVLA registration and any required IVA (Individual Vehicle Approval) testing. The full process typically takes 8–14 weeks depending on the vehicle and logistics.

Key considerations when importing from Japan

  • Age restriction: HMRC requires vehicles to be over 6 months old and have travelled more than 6,000 km to avoid paying VAT on a "new" vehicle basis.
  • IVA testing: Vehicles not previously type-approved for the UK may require Individual Vehicle Approval. This applies to many Japanese-market cars.
  • Odometer conversion: Japanese vehicles display kilometres. Ensure all paperwork reflects this accurately for DVLA purposes.
  • Customs duty: As of 2026, import duty on passenger cars from Japan is 6.5% of CIF value. The UK-Japan CEPA trade agreement may reduce this — confirm current rates at the point of import.
  • Popular models: Nissan GT-R, Toyota Land Cruiser (domestic spec), Subaru STI, Honda NSX, Mitsubishi EVO, Lexus LFA and various Porsche Japan-spec variants are frequently sought from Japanese sources.

What is RoRo Shipping? A Guide for Vehicle Exporters

Published April 2026 · UK Car Source

Roll-on Roll-off (RoRo) shipping is one of the most common and cost-effective methods for exporting cars internationally. Rather than being lifted by crane into a container, vehicles are driven directly onto a specialist cargo vessel and secured in place for the voyage — much like a multi-deck car ferry on a much larger scale.

How RoRo works

Vehicles are delivered to a designated RoRo terminal — in the UK, the main departure ports for vehicle export include Southampton, Tilbury, Liverpool and Hull. From there, they are driven aboard the vessel by trained handlers, secured with straps and lashing, and shipped to the destination port where they are driven off on arrival.

RoRo vs Container shipping — which is right for your export?

  • RoRo is typically cheaper and faster to load/unload. It suits standard passenger vehicles, SUVs and light commercial vehicles in good, running condition.
  • Container shipping offers greater security and protection, and is recommended for high-value, classic or prestige vehicles where condition is paramount. A container can be loaded and sealed with only your vehicle inside.
  • Groupage containers allow multiple vehicles to share a container, reducing cost while maintaining some additional protection over RoRo.

Destinations served by RoRo from the UK

RoRo services from UK ports cover most major global destinations, including Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand (Laem Chabang), Sri Lanka (Colombo), East Africa (Mombasa, Dar es Salaam), West Africa (Lagos, Tema), the Caribbean (Kingston, Bridgetown) and Australia/New Zealand.

Why Use a Vehicle Sourcing Agent? A Buyer's Guide

Published February 2026 · UK Car Source

The UK new and nearly-new car market has changed significantly in recent years. Supply constraints, limited dealer stock and high demand for specific models have made it harder than ever for private buyers to secure the exact vehicle they want — at the right price — through a dealer forecourt alone.

A vehicle sourcing agent acts as your professional representative in the market. Rather than visiting dealers yourself or trawling listings, an agent taps into their established network — main dealer groups, franchise contacts, specialist suppliers and auction channels — to locate your specific requirement quickly and efficiently.

When does it make sense to use a sourcing agent?

  • You want a specific model, colour or specification that isn't readily available
  • You want access to factory-order pricing without dealer wait times
  • You're importing a vehicle from outside the UK
  • You want to buy a high-value vehicle with professional due diligence on its history and condition
  • You're a trade buyer sourcing multiple vehicles and need a reliable supply partner
  • You're based overseas and need a trusted UK representative to handle a purchase and export
