Import Guide: Singapore

Importing a Salvage Car from the UK to Singapore

A repaired UK write-off can cost far less than a clean car, and in Singapore the COE and taxes are the same either way. But Singapore's rules are strict, and LTA does not publish a specific policy on UK write-off categories. Here is what to check before you buy.

Talk to Us

UK Write-Off Categories

In the UK, insurers classify a car as a write-off when repair is uneconomic or the damage is too severe to repair safely. There are four categories:

CategoryDescriptionCan it return to the road in the UK?
Category AScrap only. The whole car must be crushedNo
Category BBody shell must be crushed. Some parts can be reusedNo
Category S (formerly C)Structural damage, repaired professionallyYes, after repair
Category N (formerly D)Non-structural damage, such as cosmetic, trim or electricalYes, after repair

Only Category S and Category N cars can be repaired and used again, so these are the only categories worth considering for export.

The Singapore Rules That Apply

Singapore applies the same rules to a repaired write-off as to any other imported used car:

  • Age: the car must be no more than 3 years old at registration, counted from its first registration abroad. Many salvage cars on sale in the UK are older than this.
  • Right hand drive only, and new diesel car registrations ended on 1 January 2025. Petrol cars must meet Euro VI.
  • Inspection: the car must pass an inspection at an LTA authorised centre, and an emissions test, before it can be registered.
  • Used car surcharge: S$10,000 on top of the normal excise duty, ARF, GST, registration fee and COE.

Check Before You Buy

LTA does not publish a rule on UK write-off categories, so we cannot tell you in advance that a particular car will be registered. Before you commit, ask your Singapore importer or clearing agent, or LTA directly, whether a car with this history and repair will be accepted. A car that is refused registration has to be re-exported or disposed of, at your cost.

How Category N and Category S Compare

  • Category N: no structural damage, so the risk at inspection is lower. This is the category we source for Singapore.
  • Category S: the structure has been damaged and repaired. Expect closer scrutiny, harder insurance and lower resale value. We do not recommend Category S cars for Singapore.

Do the Numbers Still Work in 2026?

The appeal is simple: the COE, ARF and other charges do not fall because a car was once written off, so a lower purchase price goes straight to the bottom line. Excise duty, ARF and GST are based on the OMV, so a lower price also lowers those charges.

The Vehicular Emissions Scheme changes the picture for big petrol cars. In 2026 the highest emission cars pay a VES surcharge of up to S$35,000, rising to S$45,000 in 2027, and only electric cars earn a rebate. A large petrol SUV can lose much of its salvage discount to the VES. Electric cars carry their own risk: battery or high voltage damage can be expensive to put right. Work through the full costs in our Singapore cost guide before you buy.

Risks to Weigh Up

  • Registration refusal: the main risk. Confirm acceptability in Singapore before you buy.
  • Hidden damage: a car can have more damage than its category suggests. Read the auction inspection report, where one is available, and consider an independent inspection.
  • Repair costs: budget conservatively and get quotes before committing.
  • Insurance: some Singapore insurers decline salvage history cars or charge more. Check first.
  • Resale: write-off history affects resale value in Singapore, even after a good repair.

Documents Required

  • UK V5C registration certificate
  • The insurer's or auction house's document confirming the write-off category
  • Auction house inspection report, where available
  • Repair invoices and photos, where available
  • Commercial invoice and Bill of Lading
  • Marine insurance certificate (CIF terms or on request)

How UK Car Source Helps

We source Category N cars in the UK and pass on the auction house inspection report where one is available. We coordinate inspections and shipping through specialist partners, and your own clearing agent handles customs. We do not give repair estimates, and we cannot guarantee that LTA will accept a particular car, so we will be open with you about the damage and the risks.
