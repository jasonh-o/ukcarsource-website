Import Guide — Sri Lanka

How to Import a Car from the UK to Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka drives on the left, making UK right-hand drive vehicles ideal — but strict import controls, a permit system, and some of the highest effective duty rates in the world mean that importing from the UK requires careful planning and professional guidance.

Talk to Our Team

Overview — Sri Lanka and Vehicle Imports

Sri Lanka is a left-hand traffic country where RHD vehicles are the norm — the UK is therefore a natural and ideal source for Sri Lankan vehicle buyers. However, Sri Lanka maintains some of the strictest and most complex vehicle import regulations in Asia. The government has periodically imposed full import bans, strict age limits, and a permit-based system that controls who can import vehicles and how many. Import liberalisation and restriction has historically alternated with the country's economic policy cycles.

For buyers who do qualify to import — and for high-value prestige vehicles where the economics justify the substantial duty burden — the UK remains an excellent source of quality right-hand drive vehicles. Land Rovers, BMWs, Mercedes-Benz, and increasingly Rolls-Royce and Bentley vehicles have all been successfully imported from the UK to Sri Lanka.

Important Notice

Sri Lanka's vehicle import policy has historically changed frequently. The permit system, allowable vehicle types, age limits, and duty rates are all subject to government policy revision. Always verify current regulations with a Sri Lankan customs broker or the Sri Lanka Customs Department before committing to a purchase. The information below reflects the established framework; specific figures should be independently confirmed.

Import Permit System

Sri Lanka operates a controlled import permit system for motor vehicles. Free open importation of vehicles is not available to ordinary private buyers. Import permits are typically available to specific categories of people, including:

  • Returning residents and diaspora: Sri Lankan nationals who have been resident abroad for a qualifying period (typically 2+ years) may be eligible for a concessionary duty rate on a single vehicle import upon return.
  • Government officials and diplomats: May import vehicles under separate concessionary regimes.
  • Licensed vehicle importers: Commercial importers who hold relevant permits can import vehicles for resale under applicable duty rates.
  • Lease and permit auctionees: The government periodically issues import permits by lottery or auction — these can be bought and sold and are often used to bring in higher-value vehicles.

For most buyers, obtaining a vehicle import permit is the first and most critical step — without it, the vehicle cannot legally clear customs at Colombo Port. We strongly recommend engaging a Sri Lankan customs specialist before initiating any vehicle purchase in the UK.

Duty Structure — Among the World's Highest

Sri Lanka imposes a layered duty structure on imported vehicles that can result in total taxes equalling or exceeding 100% of the vehicle's CIF value for standard passenger cars, and in excess of 200% for large-engine luxury vehicles. The components include:

Duty / TaxApproximate Rate
Customs Duty20–100% (engine-size dependent)
Excise Duty30–100%+ (varies by engine displacement)
Value Added Tax (VAT)15–18%
Ports and Airports Levy (PAL)10%
Nation Building Tax (NBT)2% (varies)

For a luxury SUV like a Range Rover Autobiography at a CIF value of approximately USD 120,000, the total duty and tax burden in Sri Lanka can realistically exceed USD 150,000–180,000, resulting in a landed cost approaching USD 300,000 before registration fees. Only buyers who have access to concessionary duty permits — or where the prestige value significantly exceeds the economic logic — typically proceed at full duty rates.

Returning resident permit holders benefit from substantially reduced duty rates, which is why the majority of personal use vehicle imports to Sri Lanka are made by returning expatriates.

Age Restrictions

Sri Lanka has historically imposed age restrictions on imported vehicles. The general rule has been that passenger vehicles must not be older than a specified year at the time of import — this has varied between 3 and 5 years in recent policy cycles. The practical implication is that importers must source relatively recent vehicles from the UK, which narrows the used market pool but also ensures quality.

Always confirm the current age limit with a Sri Lankan customs broker before selecting a vehicle in the UK — purchasing a vehicle that is one year outside the permitted age window can result in it being refused clearance at Colombo Port.

Colombo Port — Entry Process

All vehicle imports to Sri Lanka enter through Colombo Port (specifically, the South Harbour / Jaya Container Terminal or the conventional port depending on the shipping arrangement). Transit time from UK ports to Colombo is typically 22–30 days by container shipping via intermediate transhipment ports (often Port Klang or Singapore).

On arrival, the vehicle must be cleared by Sri Lanka Customs. A licensed customs clearing agent in Colombo is essential — they will present all documents, pay assessed duties, and arrange vehicle release. The clearing process typically takes 5–15 working days once all documentation is in order.

Most Popular Models Imported

Despite the high duty burden, the following vehicle types are regularly imported from the UK to Sri Lanka:

  • Toyota Land Cruiser — particularly 4WD models sought for durability
  • Land Rover Defender and Discovery — both aspirational and practical
  • Range Rover — status vehicle for the affluent market in Colombo
  • BMW 5-Series and 7-Series — executive sedans with strong brand recognition
  • Mercedes-Benz E-Class and S-Class — premium saloon market
  • Rolls-Royce and Bentley — ultra-luxury for the highest tier of buyers who can access permit exemptions

Documentation Required

  • Vehicle import permit (issued by Sri Lanka Ministry of Finance / Customs)
  • UK V5C (vehicle registration document)
  • Original purchase invoice
  • Bill of Lading
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Marine insurance certificate
  • Valid MOT certificate (or equivalent roadworthiness certification)
  • Proof of importer's eligibility (e.g. returning resident documentation, employment proof)

Common Issues and How to Avoid Them

  • Permit first, vehicle second: Never purchase a vehicle in the UK before the import permit is confirmed and in hand. Permits can be revoked or restricted by policy change.
  • Age limit compliance: Confirm the current age restriction before selecting a vehicle. The manufacture date on the V5C is what matters, not the registration date.
  • Accurate CIF valuation: Sri Lanka Customs uses customs valuation rules and may refer to published price lists. Undervaluing a vehicle to reduce duty is a serious offence and can result in seizure.
  • Use a Colombo clearing agent: The Sri Lankan clearance process is complex and documents must be presented correctly. A reputable clearing agent is non-negotiable.
  • Monitor policy changes: Sri Lanka has historically suspended vehicle imports during economic or forex crises. Check current policy immediately before proceeding.
