Overview

Exporting to Nigeria

Nigeria is one of West Africa's largest vehicle import markets. Lagos Port (Apapa) is the primary entry point for vehicle imports. Most vehicles are subject to import duty calculated on CIF value, with regulations managed by the Nigeria Customs Service.

UK Car Source manages the full export process including vehicle sourcing, pre-export inspection, documentation, sea freight booking and customs clearance support. Both RoRo and container shipping options are available.

Port Guide

Port Guide

Apapa Port (Lagos) is the primary vehicle import port, handling the vast majority of UK car imports. Tin Can Island Port (Lagos) is the secondary option and often less congested. Port Harcourt Port serves the Niger Delta region.

Apapa is notoriously congested — clearance can take 3–8 weeks with significant trucking queue delays. Pre-arrival documentation (Form M, SON IECAS registration) must be in place before shipment or goods will be held. Use a reputable clearing agent in Lagos — this is essential, not optional. NCS (Nigeria Customs Service) handles all formal clearance. Recommend using an experienced Nigeria-specialist freight forwarder.

Market Advice

Local Market Advice

Lagos is one of Africa's largest prestige car markets. Nigerian buyers are extremely brand-conscious — condition, specification, and provenance matter enormously. Low mileage is essential; anything over 60,000km is considered high mileage. Black, white, and silver dominate colour preferences — avoid unusual colours.

The G63 AMG commands a significant premium in Lagos. Many buyers source through agents to avoid inflated local dealer prices. Budget for customs agent fees of 5–8% of vehicle value on top of duties. The top end is dominated by ultra-luxury models — the market for prestige vehicles remains robust even amid economic uncertainty.
